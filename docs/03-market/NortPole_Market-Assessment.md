# NortPole — market assessment

Status: **discovery plan; no market-size claim** · Updated: 2026-11-05

## Market question

Which organization has a frequent, costly workflow that can be improved by evidence-based agents, can provide lawful access to the necessary data, and has a buyer willing to fund a pilot?

Earlier chats named life sciences, legal, finance, sales, engineering, and research as possibilities. They are candidate segments, not a ranked market conclusion. A top-down market-size estimate would add little until the buyer, task frequency, value, and sale motion are known.

## Segment selection criteria

Score each candidate from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong) using interview evidence. Do not fill scores from intuition alone.

| Criterion | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- |
| Pain and frequency | Monthly task count, current time per task, backlog, error and rework cost. |
| Budget and urgency | Named buyer, existing spend, decision window, willingness to run a paid pilot. |
| Workflow clarity | Consistent inputs, outputs, review points, and success standard. |
| Data access | Source owner, permissions, export path, data class, retention obligations. |
| Feasibility | Ability to produce useful output with inspectable evidence and bounded actions. |
| Distribution | Reachable design partners and a repeatable acquisition channel. |
| Expansion | Adjacent workflows that can reuse the same controls and integrations. |

## Candidate wedges

| Candidate | Illustrative task | Primary unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research or life sciences | Evidence-backed literature report. | Access to reliable sources and expert review standard. |
| Legal operations | Contract issue summary for a human reviewer. | Permission, confidentiality, and buyer trust. |
| Finance operations | Evidence-backed exception investigation. | System access, audit requirements, and action controls. |
| Sales operations | Account research and approved CRM updates. | Whether current tools already solve enough of the workflow. |
| Engineering operations | Incident evidence synthesis and handoff. | Integration depth and on-call reliability requirements. |

These examples are discovery prompts, not promised product lines.

## Discovery method

Interview at least two practitioners, one workflow owner, and one economic buyer per serious segment. Observe the current task where possible. Ask for recent examples, actual time spent, errors, approvals, source systems, and what happens when output is wrong. Request a pilot commitment after the problem and acceptance standard are clear.

## Pilot economics worksheet

For a selected workflow, estimate monthly value from task volume × time saved × loaded labor rate, plus measurable avoided rework or faster cycle time. Subtract model, infrastructure, integration, review, support, and sales costs. Keep assumptions visible and update with pilot measurements.

## Interview prompt for buyers considering an enterprise AI workspace

Ask a team to bring its last completed deliverable and walk through retrieval, verification, revision, and sign-off. Record who spent time at each step, what the current workspace already handles, and which failure would prevent adoption. Ask whether improving this workflow would justify a funded pilot alongside the buyer's existing tools.

## Decision gate

Choose one segment only when there is a named buyer, representative workflow data, an agreed quality standard, a baseline, accessible users, and a realistic path to a paid pilot. Otherwise continue discovery rather than expanding the platform scope.

## Vertical offer

Select a pilot workflow for a named buyer and team. The vertical pack supplies the workflow, reviewer rules and quality cases. Evaluate willingness to pay, repeated use and review effort before expanding.
